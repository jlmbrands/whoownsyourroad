About this project

An independent look at who controls public infrastructure in Texas, starting with the toll lanes North Texans drive every day.

Who we are

Who Owns Your Road was started by a North Texas driver who got tired of paying surge tolls without knowing where the money went. It isn't funded by, affiliated with, or working for any company, political party or campaign.

How we check facts

We only publish figures we can trace to a government record, a company's own statements or filings, or reporting by an established news outlet. Every figure links to its source. Ownership stakes can change through sales that aren't always announced, so we note the date of each figure and update it when newer filings appear.

We show both sides. These contracts rebuilt free lanes, moved financial risk off the state and return the roads to Texas in 2061. We think drivers deserve the full picture, not just the parts that make people angry.

What's next

Toll roads are the start. We plan to cover other public infrastructure in Texas run under long private contracts, including utilities, water systems, ports and pipelines.

Privacy

When you sign the petition we collect your first and last name, email address, ZIP code, an optional comment, and whether you want updates.

  • Your name and ZIP code may be shared with your own state legislators when we deliver the petition.
  • Comments may be quoted without your name.
  • Your email is used only to send updates you asked for. It is never sold, rented or shared.
  • We don't use advertising trackers or analytics cookies.
  • To have your signature removed, email us from the address you signed with.

Who Owns Your Road is an independent project. It isn't affiliated with TxDOT, TEXpress Lanes, Ferrovial or any campaign.
