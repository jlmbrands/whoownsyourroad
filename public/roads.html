How the price gets to $20

Toll prices on these lanes change with traffic and are recalculated every five minutes, around the clock.13 The goal written into the contracts is to keep the toll lanes moving at a minimum of 50 mph. When NBC 5 DFW looked into a $24 posted toll on the NTE, the operator said there was a normal cap of about $1.06 per mile, but the contract with TxDOT lets prices go past it when needed to hold that speed.12

Ferrovial calls those high-demand periods "mandatory mode," and it has told investors that more time in mandatory mode helped lift revenue per trip.17 In 2025, revenue per trip grew far faster than inflation on all three roads:15

What Texas got in return

A fair accounting has to include the other side. These deals rebuilt the free main lanes and frontage roads on all three corridors, years earlier than state funding alone would have. Investors carry the risk if traffic falls short, which matters: another Cintra-led toll road, SH 130 near Austin, went bankrupt when revenue missed projections.21 The state shares in revenue above set levels, and the roads come back fully to TxDOT when the contracts end.16

The question is whether that trade still makes sense for drivers paying surge prices today, and what the public should be allowed to know and change for the next 35 years.
